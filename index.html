About Me

I am a PhD researcher in Political Science at Griffith University, Brisbane, Australia.

My research lies at the intersection of political sociology, political psychology, comparative politics, and public opinion. I am particularly interested in how citizens form judgements about political institutions and how different forms of reasoning, identity, information environments, and political communication influence institutional trust.

My broader research interests include democratic backsliding, elite politics, authoritarian politics, political communication, social movements, and computational social science.

Research

Political Trust

Public Trust and Political Judgement

My PhD research investigates how citizens form judgements about the trustworthiness of political institutions.

I examine how different forms of rationality, cognitive reasoning, identity, information environments, and framing shape trust decisions.

Elite Politics

Political Elite Networks in Hong Kong

This project examines changes in the organisational foundations of political elite coordination in Hong Kong.

Using network analysis of Legislative Council candidates and elected elites, I study how political and organisational networks changed between 2012 and 2021.

Political Behaviour

Rationality, Identity and Political Behaviour

I am interested in understanding when citizens rely on instrumental evaluations and when political judgements instead reflect values, identities, emotions, and social belonging.
